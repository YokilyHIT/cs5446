1.Research Questions

World-Model Guidance → Predictive Guidance → Missing Information → Actionable Guidance

RQ1 — WHEN

When is conventional predictive world-model guidance useful, and can it be selectively invoked?

RQ2 — WHAT

What information makes world-model guidance useful to the planner?

RQ3 — HOW

How can a frozen world model generate actionable guidance without explicit environment rules?

Secondary Question

How does planner capability change the value of diagnostic foresight?

2.Motivation

LLM agents often fail in long-horizon environments because they choose infeasible actions, miss action preconditions, lose track of state, or repeat failed behavior. A natural solution is to use a world model to predict future outcomes.

A common way to expose world-model knowledge is predictive guidance: predict the next observation or future state and provide it to the planner. We begin from this standard interface before considering other forms of guidance.

However, future prediction only helps if the planner can convert it into a better decision.

Prediction Accuracy ≠ Decision Utility

The project therefore studies not only whether foresight is accurate, but whether its information is useful for action selection.

3.Study 1 — WHEN: When Is Predictive Guidance Useful?

Starting Point

We first evaluate the standard predictive interface: next-observation foresight. We test both its overall value and whether selective invocation can improve its cost-effectiveness.

The initial hypothesis is that predictive guidance may be useful only in selected states. If so, a router should identify those states and allocate it selectively.

state → router → use predictive foresight / do not use predictive foresight

4.Why Predictive Guidance Is Not Enough

The routing results suggest a deeper issue: the main limitation may not be when predictive guidance is invoked, but whether next-observation prediction is the information the planner actually needs.

State-level intervention results support this interpretation: the incremental benefit of W4 or W14 over resampling is close to zero.

Project transition: after testing the standard predictive interface, we ask a broader question: what information should the world model expose to the planner?

7.Designing Actionable World-Model Guidance

Based on Study 2, we redesign the world-model interface from raw prediction to decision-relevant diagnosis and repair.

Predictive Guidance

Fpred(ht, at) → predicted next observation

Question: What will happen?

Actionable Guidance

Actual frozen trigger: before execution, check whether the proposed action is absent from the environment-provided admissible-action list (excluding the check-valid-actions command). Only then ask Qwen3-14B, thinking off, for diagnosis and repair guidance and let the planner revise its action. No hand-written rule cards are supplied to this arm, but it does rely on environment-provided legality information.

Frepair(ht, at, invalid(at)) → {failure cause, missing precondition, first repair action}

Questions: Why is the proposed action infeasible? What condition is missing? What should be done first?

The world model remains the source of environment knowledge. The change is the guidance interface: from outcome prediction to actionable diagnosis and repair.

9.Capability-Dependent Behavior

The final evaluation sharpens the capability question: does diagnostic guidance contribute knowledge beyond what the planner can already use?

4B Planner: substantial additional value

On fresh600, failure-only reaches 28.3% SR; frozen 14B WM repair reaches 36.2%. It also exceeds generic reflection (28.6%), structured reflection (23.7%), and rule cards (33.3%). A 4B WM reaches only 29.7%.

14B Planner: explicit rules remain strong

On fresh600, failure-only reaches 63.9% SR and WM repair 65.9%; rule cards reach 68.8% and oracle repair 69.5%. WM repair is not a universally superior guidance source.

Finding 5: diagnostic repair has greater incremental value for the weaker planner in these experiments. For the stronger planner, precise rule cards outperform the frozen WM on fresh600.
Interpretation, not an identified mechanism: knowledge overlap between the 14B planner and WM could explain the smaller gain. The earlier state-tracking explanation remains a hypothesis: the oracle gap does not isolate state observability from rule knowledge, diagnosis quality, or action choice. These are secondary comparisons, not a pre-registered statistical interaction test.

10.Frozen Evaluation: Does the Repair Story Hold?

10.1 From development to a frozen test

After Stage A, the method, prompts, decoding, code hashes, and test-set identifier were frozen on 2026-10-21. The selected method is inv_wmrepair with Qwen3-14B WM, thinking off. Fresh600 contains 600 previously unrun train-split task instances, each evaluated three times. It tests new instances in the same scene distribution, not a new environment distribution. Unseen134 is a separate out-of-distribution split evaluated five times, but has been used in earlier rounds and is treated as secondary repeated evaluation.

SR measures full-task success; PR measures subgoal progress. Inference averages repetitions within each task, then uses 20,000 paired task-bootstrap samples. Thus fresh600 has 600 task units, not 1,800 independent tasks. All differences below are percentage points (pp), with 95% confidence intervals.

10.3 What the controls add to the story

  • Failure detection already helps. Failure-only raises 4B SR by 7.4 pp over none. WM repair adds a further 7.9 pp [5.7, 10.1]. The total gain cannot all be attributed to diagnosis.
  • Extra reflection alone is insufficient. Generic reflection is close to failure-only; structured reflection is worse in this configuration. WM repair exceeds them by 7.6 and 12.6 pp SR respectively. This constrains the “just think again” explanation without proving that all reflection methods are ineffective.
  • Diagnostic model capability matters. The 4B WM trails the 14B WM by 6.6 pp SR; its gain over failure-only is not significant. A separate diagnostic role alone does not explain the effect, and model capability is not held constant in this comparison.
  • Rule cards are a substantive comparator. Frozen repair exceeds them by 2.9 pp SR [0.4, 5.4] for 4B, an exploratory superiority result. For 14B, it trails rule cards by 2.9 pp [-5.1, -0.8]. These are hand-written cards, not a reproduction of a full external rule-retrieval system.
  • Near the oracle comparator, not proven equivalent. For 4B, oracle minus frozen repair is +0.4 pp SR [-1.8, 2.7]. An undetected difference is not an equivalence test, and oracle diagnosis is not an optimal-policy ceiling.

10.5 Cost and scope

On fresh600 with a 4B planner, frozen repair uses 47.8k tokens/task: about 27% above none, 2% above failure-only, and 8% above rule cards. Tokens aggregate planner and WM inputs/outputs; mixed model sizes mean this is not a latency, monetary-cost, or compute-matched comparison. A 14B planner alone achieves 59.1% SR with 27.5k tokens, so these results do not establish 4B-plus-WM as the best deployment configuration.

Boundaries: the method depends on an admissible-action gate. Fresh600 does not directly repeat all predictive-interface arms from Study 2. The prediction-to-repair argument combines development interventions with frozen validation of the selected repair method; it is not a fresh600 head-to-head result against every prediction baseline. Absolute dev/test rates should not be compared as treatment effects.

10.6 Remaining experiments

Reflection controls, rule-card controls, and frozen evaluation are now complete. Open questions are: separately ablate cause, missing precondition, and first repair action; test counterfactual feasibility guidance; compare state-information interventions for the stronger planner; evaluate learned or post-execution triggers without an admissible-action gate; and compare models under matched compute or latency. These remain proposals, not findings of p7_final.

12.Contribution Framing

Weak Claim

We use a world model to repair failed actions.

Better Claim

We use a pre-execution invalid-action signal to trigger targeted frozen-world-model diagnosis and repair.

Strongest Conceptual Claim

Our experiments motivate a shift from selecting when to predict toward designing decision-relevant diagnostic guidance; frozen evaluation confirms its value for a weaker planner under an admissible-action gate.

In this project, raw outcome prediction is weak, while decision-relevant abstractions such as missing preconditions and first repair actions are substantially more useful.

14.Suggested Abstract Logic

  1. World models are commonly used to provide predictive guidance about future outcomes to LLM agents.
  2. We first test whether its value can be improved by learning when to invoke it.
  3. Selective routing gives only weak gains and does not reliably outperform random selection.
  4. Failure analysis shows that many errors come from infeasible actions and missing preconditions.
  5. Controlled interventions show that true next observations are much less useful than explicit failure diagnosis and repair guidance.
  6. We therefore study repair-oriented foresight, where an invalid proposed action triggers a frozen world model to diagnose missing preconditions and suggest a first repair action before execution.
  7. In pre-registered fresh600 evaluation, frozen 14B WM repair raises 4B planner SR from 20.9% to 36.2%, improves PR over failure-only by 4.2 pp, and meets PR non-inferiority to rule cards. Its observed SR is close to the oracle-diagnosis comparator, without establishing equivalence.
  8. Benefits depend on planner capability: rule cards outperform WM repair for the 14B planner on fresh600. Repeated unseen134 evaluation supports the 4B trend, with uncertainty in PR contrasts. The results support targeted diagnostic guidance within this protocol, rather than a universal advantage of WM repair.

15.Final Message

Recommended takeaway:

World-model guidance should not be reduced to future-state prediction. Its value depends on whether the information exposed to the planner is decision-relevant. Development probes favor diagnosis and repair over raw next-observation prediction. Frozen evaluation confirms that a strong diagnostic WM improves a weaker planner beyond failure reminders; explicit rule cards remain stronger for the 14B planner on fresh600. This conclusion is conditional on the tested models, tasks, and pre-execution legality gate.
One-line conclusion:
Effective world-model guidance is not only about predicting the future; it is about exposing the right world knowledge for the planner's next decision.
